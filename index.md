## Quarter 2 Project Brainstorming

**1. What is the most interesting topic covered in your domain this quarter?**

The most interesting topic I covered in my domain this quarter was learning how visualizations and simulations can help students better understand statistical concepts. I found statistics such as Exponential and Gamma distribution concepts difficult to understand through formulas alone but became easier to interpret when students can experiment with different parameters and observe how the distribution changes. 

**2. Describe a potential investigation you would like to pursue for your Quarter 2 Project.**

I would like ot investigate how to improve interactive exercises and visualizations to help students' understanding of probability distributions compared to traditional exercises. One possible approach would be to create a different version of a Jupyter Notebook with one focusing on mathematical explanations and another including simulations, interactive plots, and real world examples. I could then compare student feedback and performance on conceptual questions to determine which approach is more effective.

**3. What is a potential change you'd make to the approach taken in your current Quarter 1 Project?**

One change I would make is to focus more on testig how effective our exercises are from a student's perspective. While we have been working on improving instructions, adding visualizations, and creating exercises, I think it would be more useful to have other students try them out and provide feedback. This would help us identify confusing instructions or concepts and make improvements based on students' actual experiences rather than our own assumptions or thoughts.

**4. What other techniques would you be interested in using in your project?**

I would be more interested in using more interactive visualizations, simulations, and statistical analysis in our project. One library I want to use is ipywidgets which could allow students to change distribution parameters and immediately see how results change. I would also like to explore using hypothesis testing or other statistical techniques to evalute whether the updated exercises actually improve students' understanding of the material.
